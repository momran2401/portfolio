FAA UAS Test Site, Texas A&M Autonomy Research Institute/Corpus Christi, TX/May to Sep 2025

Field-ready LiDAR measurement device

A rugged measuring tool that 20 engineers and field users at the FAA test site picked up and used.

  • Raspberry Pi
  • Arduino
  • Python
  • C++
  • IMU
  • Calibration
  • Self-tests
  • 3D printing

The goal

The Autonomy Research Institute in Corpus Christi is one of seven FAA UAS test sites in the country. The engineers there needed a LiDAR tool for taking measurements in the field, and it had to work for people who didn't build it. Working on my desk wasn't enough. It had to tell you when something was wrong, and it had to hold up out there.

What's inside

  • Raspberry Pi and Arduino, with about 2,000 lines of Python and C++ between them.
  • An IMU that tracks orientation and guides your aim, so you know you're pointed the right way before you take a reading.
  • Two 16x2 LCDs as the interface. No laptop needed.
  • Redundant logging to an SD card and to the cloud, so one failing doesn't cost you the data.
  • A rugged 3D-printed enclosure with a wiring layout you can actually open up and service.

Making it trustworthy

A measurement tool is only useful if you believe the number. So I built in calibration and sensor self-tests. On startup, the device runs fault checks on its sensors and connections. If something's unplugged or not responding, it tells you what's wrong and how to fix it before you start measuring.

I also wrote the setup, calibration, and troubleshooting docs, so someone in the field could run it, and fix it, without calling me.
